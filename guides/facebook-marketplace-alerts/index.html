How to Get Instant Facebook Marketplace Alerts

If you've ever watched a great deal sell 20 minutes after it was listed — before Facebook even told you it existed — you already know the problem. Facebook Marketplace does have alerts, but they're slow and unreliable. This guide covers how the native option works, why it fails, and how to get alerted in seconds instead.

Option 1: Facebook's built-in saved search alerts

On Marketplace, search for what you want, then tap the bell or "Save search" and enable notifications. That's the only official option — and it has three big problems:

  • They're slow. Saved-search notifications routinely arrive hours after the listing went live. Good deals sell in minutes.
  • They're throttled. Marketplace alerts sit in the same queue as likes, comments and group activity — Facebook's algorithm decides if and when you see them.
  • They miss listings. Many matching listings simply never trigger a notification.

Option 2: a dedicated Marketplace alerts app

Dedicated monitoring apps work differently: they check public Marketplace listings on a fixed schedule — continuously, around the clock — and push an alert the moment a new match appears. The only number that matters is the check interval: an app that checks every 15 seconds will beat one that checks every 5 minutes to every hot deal.

Setting up instant alerts with Zoopy

  1. Create a hunt. Enter keywords (e.g. "iPhone 14 Pro"), a price range, and any suburb plus a radius. iPhones get per-model price bands; cars get make/model/year/km filters.
  2. Pick a check speed. Five paces, from every 15 minutes (Relaxed) down to every 15 seconds (Turbo). Use Turbo for competitive items where seconds decide who wins.
  3. Turn on lock-screen alerts. Every alert arrives with photo, price, distance and an instant valuation against comparable listings — so you know whether to sprint.
Tip: also create hunts for phrases motivated sellers use — "moving sale", "must go", "need gone" — they're where the underpriced listings hide.

What makes an alert actually useful

Speed alone isn't enough. A useful alert also needs filtering (Zoopy hides dealers, damaged, salvage and written-off stock automatically, even when it's only disclosed in the description) and context (a valuation against the market, so you don't waste first-mover advantage on an overpriced listing).
