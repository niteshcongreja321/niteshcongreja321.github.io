How to get Facebook Marketplace alerts

How to set up Facebook's own Notify Me alerts on desktop, iPhone and Android, where they fall short, and how an alert app watches your searches for you.

By the Zoopy team Updated 26 September 2026 9 min read

Quick answer

Yes, Facebook Marketplace has alerts. On a computer, search Marketplace and click Notify Me under Search results. In the app, save the search where your version offers it, then check that saved-search notifications are switched on in Marketplace’s settings. For round-the-clock checks with finer filters, use a dedicated alert app.

Does Facebook Marketplace have alerts?

Yes. Facebook calls them saved searches. You search for something, save the search, and Facebook can notify you when new listings match it. When Facebook launched the feature, it pitched it as a way to get updates “when there’s new stuff you want”.

Saved-search alerts are one kind of Marketplace notification among several. Facebook’s help centre lists the categories you can turn on or off in Marketplace settings, giving updates, saved searches and tips for sellers as examples. Messages from buyers and sellers are different again: they arrive in your Marketplace inbox and in Messenger, with their own settings.

Two honest caveats before you rely on Facebook’s alerts:

  • At the time of writing (September 2026), we couldn’t find a Facebook help centre page with step-by-step instructions for creating an alert, and the buttons differ between the website and the apps. Below is what we could confirm, and we say where we couldn’t.
  • Facebook doesn’t publish how soon after a listing appears it sends the notification.

How to set up a Marketplace alert on a computer

These steps combine Facebook’s help centre with what we saw on facebook.com on 26 September 2026.

  1. Open facebook.com and click Marketplace in the left menu.
  2. Type what you want in the search box and press Enter. Be specific: “Makita 18V drill” gets you closer than “drill”.
  3. Set where to look. Below Filters, click the location, enter a place and the distance you’re willing to travel, then click Apply.
  4. Add a minimum and maximum under Price, so the alert skips things you would never buy.
  5. In the left-hand panel, under Search results, click Notify Me.
  6. Follow the prompts to create the alert. In How-To Geek’s walkthrough (last updated June 2024), this step also let you set a price range and radius for the alert.

Then make sure Facebook is allowed to tell you. Click your profile picture in the top right, then Settings and privacy, Settings, Notifications and Marketplace, and switch on the notification types you want.

How to set up a Marketplace alert on iPhone or Android

Facebook’s help pages confirm how to search and where the notification switches live, but they don’t describe the save button itself, which varies between app versions and regions.

  1. Tap Menu, then Marketplace. If you can’t see it, tap See more. If Marketplace is a shortcut in your tab bar, tap that instead.
  2. Tap Search, type what you want, then tap Search again.
  3. To change the area, tap the location on the right, move the map or search for a new place, tap Custom local radius, drag the slider, then tap Apply.
  4. Tap Filters to set a price range and other filters, then tap See items.
  5. On the results screen, look for an option to save the search or to be notified about new listings. If your version doesn’t show one, create the alert on a computer instead, then check that it reaches your phone.
  6. Switch the alerts on: tap Menu, Marketplace, your Marketplace profile, Settings, then Notifications. Tap the saved searches category and turn its toggle on. Facebook’s help says blue means on and grey means off.
  7. Check your phone lets Facebook notify you. On iPhone: Settings > Notifications > Facebook > Allow Notifications. On Android: Settings > Notifications > App notifications, then Facebook. Labels vary by phone maker.

Another Facebook option: follow a seller

Facebook’s help centre documents one more way to hear about new listings: follow the seller. Facebook says that when you follow someone on Marketplace, you’ll get notifications when they create new listings. It suits sellers who list the same kind of thing again and again, such as someone who restores bikes or builds furniture.

  • In the app: open one of their listings, scroll down to Seller information and tap Follow next to their name. To unfollow, go to Marketplace, then Profile, then Following.
  • On a computer: open one of their listings, click the seller’s name, then click Follow. To unfollow, go to Marketplace, then Buying, then Following.

Facebook notes that the seller is notified when you follow them, though not when you unfollow.

What Facebook’s own alerts can’t do

Facebook’s alerts cost nothing, and for a casual search they may be all you need. Their limits show when you’re hunting for something specific.

  • One place per search. Each search covers one location and radius, and Facebook’s help centre says the current maximum is 100 miles or kilometres. Covering a region means several searches. Our guide to searching a wide area shows how.
  • Loose matching, and no way to leave words out. Marketplace search matches loosely. When we searched “couch -leather” around Brisbane, 23 of the 24 newest results were leather couches, against 7 of 24 for a plain “couch” search. The minus sign pulled leather in rather than keeping it out. More tests are in our search tips.
  • No say over timing. You can’t choose how often Facebook checks your search, and it doesn’t say how quickly it notifies you. In Facebook’s own forums, users report saved-search alerts that stopped arriving and saved searches that wouldn’t delete.
  • Muting Facebook mutes your alerts. Your Marketplace alerts come from the Facebook app, so if you turn Facebook’s notifications down to escape the noise, the alerts go quiet too.

If your alerts have simply stopped, our notifications troubleshooting guide has iPhone and Android checklists.

How do Marketplace alert apps work?

A dedicated alert app does the refreshing for you, from its own computers rather than your phone.

  1. It reads public listings. Marketplace search results are public. We opened searches for Brisbane, Sydney and Toronto in a browser without logging in and could see the listings, with their titles, prices and suburbs.
  2. It runs your search on a schedule, day and night, and spots listings it hasn’t seen before.
  3. It checks each new listing against your filters, such as price, distance and words to include or avoid. Better apps open the listing itself, so the filters see the description as well as the title.
  4. It sends a push notification from its own app. You open the listing and message the seller yourself.

Why they don’t need your Facebook login

Because they read the same public pages anyone can see without signing in, a well-built alert app never needs your Facebook password or access to your account. It can’t post, read your messages or contact sellers for you. Facebook’s security advice is never to share your password with anyone, so be wary of any tool that asks you to type it in.

What decides how fast any alert can be

In our measurements, Facebook keeps a new listing out of public search for about six to seven minutes after the seller posts it. That wait is the same for everyone searching, including every alert app and anyone refreshing by hand. An app that checks very often still can’t see a listing before Facebook shows it. What differs between apps is how soon after that point they check your area, how many new listings they catch, and how well they filter out the noise.

Questions people ask

Does Facebook Marketplace have alerts?

Yes. Facebook calls them saved searches. On a computer, Marketplace search results have a Notify Me button, and Marketplace's notification settings include a saved searches category you can switch on or off.

Where do Facebook Marketplace alerts show up?

As Facebook notifications: in the notifications list inside Facebook and, if your phone allows it, as push notifications on your lock screen. To find, change or delete the searches behind them, see our saved searches guide.

Why am I not getting Marketplace alerts?

Check the settings first: phone notifications for Facebook may be off, a Focus or Do Not Disturb mode may be silencing them, battery saving may be holding Facebook back, or saved-search notifications may be off inside Marketplace. Work through our notifications checklist.

Do Marketplace alert apps need my Facebook login?

Apps that read public listings do not. Anyone can open Marketplace search results in a browser without signing in, and that is what these apps read. Facebook's own advice is never to share your password, so do not type it into any alert app.

How quickly can a Marketplace alert reach me?

In our measurements, Facebook keeps a brand-new listing out of public search for about six to seven minutes after it is posted, for everyone. No app that reads public search can see it sooner, so compare apps on what they do after that point.
