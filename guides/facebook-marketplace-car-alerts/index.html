Car alerts on Facebook Marketplace

Set up Facebook Marketplace alerts for a specific car: make, model, year, kilometres, price, radius and words to hide, with a worked example hunt.

By the Zoopy team Updated 26 September 2026 8 min read

Quick answer

To get alerts for a specific car on Facebook Marketplace, search Vehicles by make and model, narrow it by year, kilometres, price and distance, and sort by date listed. For alerts that keep running while your phone is in your pocket, use an alert app that checks that search on a schedule and reads the whole listing before it pings you.

What makes a good car alert?

A good car alert is narrow enough that every ping is worth a look, and wide enough that you do not filter out the car you want. Most bad alerts fail one of two ways.

They match words, not cars. A keyword search for “Corolla” can turn up seat covers, alloy wheels and parts as well as cars, and “Ranger” can match anything with Ranger in the title, not only Ford utes. A car alert should use structured fields (make, model, year) so it only matches vehicle listings.

They stop at the title. A title like “2016 Mazda 3 Maxx, low kms” tells you almost nothing. The words that matter (“repairable write-off”, “no rego”, “finance available”, “needs a gearbox”) are usually in the description. An alert that only reads titles cannot hide those listings for you.

Facebook’s own Vehicles filters are a good place to start. When we checked the web version of Marketplace on 26 September 2026 (logged out, in Brisbane and New York), the Vehicles category offered these filters: Sort by, Price, Vehicle type, Year, Make, Model, Mileage and Transmission type. Facebook’s Help Centre also documents sorting any search by Date listed. Labels vary between the app and the website, and between countries.

A worked example: a small hatch around Ipswich

Say you want a reliable automatic hatch for a new driver, you live in Ipswich in south-east Queensland, and your budget is about A$15,000. Here is how we would set the hunt up, and why.

Setting Value Why
Makes and models Toyota Corolla, Mazda 3 Two cars that do the same job double your choice without adding noise
Years 2015 to 2020 Move the window to suit your budget: newer costs more, older means more kilometres
Kilometres Up to 150,000 km Leaves room for well-kept high-mileage cars; drop it to 120,000 if you get too many
Gearbox Automatic A learner who will drive an automatic does not need manual listings
Price A$9,000 to A$18,000 Set the ceiling a little above budget so you see cars you can negotiate on
Place and radius Ipswich, 50 km Reaches most of Brisbane and Logan, but not the Gold Coast
Hide if it mentions wrecking, for parts, repairable write-off, statutory, hail damage, no rego, needs engine These are cars you would never buy for a new driver

Three habits make this work.

Start wide, then tighten. Run the hunt for a few days and look at what comes through. If half the alerts are 2015 cars with 148,000 km, lift the year to 2016 or drop the kilometres. It is easier to see the right settings in real listings than to guess them up front.

Hide words, not whole categories. It is tempting to hide “hail”, but “no hail damage” and “garaged, never seen hail” are good signs, and a simple word filter cannot tell them apart. Hide the phrases that only a problem car would use: “hail damaged”, “repairable write-off”, “needs engine”.

Give two areas their own hunts. If you would buy in Ipswich or on the Sunshine Coast but not in between, two smaller circles beat one huge one. You see fewer irrelevant cars and every distance on the alert means something.

For readers in miles: the same logic works for a 2016 to 2019 Honda Civic under 90,000 miles, $9,000 to $15,000, within 30 miles of home. Only the units change.

What a car alert should show you

An alert is only useful if you can decide from the lock screen whether to open it. For a car, that means the price, the kilometres, the gearbox, the fuel type and how far away it is. Anything less and you open every alert to find out.

Why reading the full listing matters

Facebook’s search results show a photo, a title, a price and a place. The seller’s description is where the car’s real story sits, and it is often the only place that says:

  • Write-off history. “Repairable write-off”, “WOVR”, “statutory”, “rebuilt title” or “Cat S”. See how to spot written-off and salvage cars.
  • Paperwork gaps. “No rego”, “unregistered”, “RWC not included”, “sold as is”.
  • Mechanical problems. “Needs a gearbox”, “engine light on”, “timing chain noise”, “blown head gasket”.
  • Dealer wording. “Finance available”, “trade-ins welcome”, “drive away”, a stock number or a dealer licence number. See how to filter out dealers.
  • Price signals. “Negotiable”, “ONO”, “price firm”, “need gone this week”.

An alert tool that reads only the title can match on none of that. One that opens the listing first can hide “repairable write-off” even when the title just says “2016 Mazda 3, drives well”. Zoopy reads the full listing before it alerts you, so your filters see the description, not just the title.

Two limits apply to any tool. First, a filter can only act on what the seller wrote: a seller who leaves out the hail damage will not be caught by a hail filter. Second, word filters are blunt. Treat them as a first pass that saves you reading, never as a history check.

Before you message the seller

A few minutes of checking before you reply saves wasted trips. Run through this for every car that makes it past your alert:

  1. Read the whole description and every photo. Look for write-off words, dealer wording and repairs in the pictures.
  2. Check the price against similar cars. Our guide to checking a Marketplace price walks through it.
  3. Look at the seller’s other listings. Five cars on one profile often means a trader.
  4. Ask for the VIN. In Australia, a $2 PPSR search on the VIN tells you if the car is recorded as having money owing, stolen or written off. The Australian car buying checklist covers every step from rego checks to the transfer.
  5. Keep payment until you have seen the car. Deposits for a car you have not inspected are a classic scam; see our guide to Marketplace scams.

Write your first message before the alert arrives

Have a short, specific message ready: “Hi, is the Corolla still available? I can see it today after 4 pm. Is the rego current and do you have the service book?” A clear message with a time in it is easier for a seller to say yes to than “is this available?”.

Questions people ask

Can Facebook Marketplace alert me when a specific car is listed?

Facebook lets you search Vehicles by make, model, year, mileage, price and gearbox and sort by date listed. Its own notifications are covered in our Marketplace alerts guide. An alert app runs your search on a schedule and pushes each new match to your phone.

Should I search by keyword or by make and model?

Make and model. A keyword like Corolla also finds seat covers, wheels and parts. Structured make, model and year fields only match car listings, and you can then hide the junk words that still slip in.

Do I need to give an alert app my Facebook login?

Not with Zoopy. It reads public Marketplace listings, and you message the seller yourself in Facebook.

Can I set car alerts in miles instead of kilometres?

Yes. Zoopy lets you set the odometer range and distances in kilometres or miles, and shows each price in the listing's own currency.

Will a car alert show me dealers and write-offs?

It depends on the tool. Zoopy can hide dealer, salvage and damaged listings from your alerts on every plan, based on the words in the listing. See our guides to filtering out dealers and spotting write-offs.
