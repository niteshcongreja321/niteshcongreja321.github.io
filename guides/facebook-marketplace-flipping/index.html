Flipping on Facebook Marketplace: The Complete Guide

Flipping on Facebook Marketplace is a simple business: buy under market, sell at market, keep the difference. The hard part isn't selling — it's sourcing: finding underpriced stock before the other twenty flippers in your city. That's a speed and information game, and it's exactly what this guide covers.

What actually flips well

  • iPhones and consoles — deep, liquid markets; prices are well-known, so mispricing is easy to spot and quick to exit.
  • Power tools — tradies sell fast when they upgrade; strong brand premiums (Milwaukee, Makita, DeWalt).
  • Furniture with keywords — "mid-century", "solid timber", "Parker" style pieces listed generically as "old table".
  • Cars — the biggest margins and the fastest competition; see the car alerts guide.

The three flipper problems (and their fixes)

1. Seeing listings first

Margin disappears in minutes. Run always-on hunts with a short check interval — Zoopy's Pro checks every 30 seconds and Max every 15. Alerts hit your lock screen while the listing is fresh, so you're the first message, not the twentieth.

2. Valuing on the spot

The classic mistake is tab-hopping to sold listings while someone else closes the deal. Zoopy prices every catch against comparable listings the moment it alerts — you see "$210 under market" on the notification itself. You can even set alerts to fire only above a profit threshold you choose.

3. Filtering junk

Half of "cheap" listings are dealers, damaged goods or scams. Zoopy hides dealer stock, damaged/salvage/written-off items (even when it's only mentioned in the description) and obvious scam patterns automatically.

A realistic starting system

  1. Pick two categories you know well.
  2. Create 4–6 hunts: the exact models you want, plus "moving sale" style motivated-seller phrases, in a 25 km radius.
  3. Set profitable-only alerts (e.g. profit ≥ $80) so your phone only buzzes when it matters.
  4. Reply within the first minute with a concrete cash-and-pickup offer.
ROI math: one $100-under-market catch covers months of any Zoopy plan. Serious flippers aren't paying for an app — they're paying for position in the queue.
