How to Get Price Drop Alerts on Facebook Marketplace

Not every deal has to be won in the first minute. Plenty of listings start overpriced, sit for a week, then drop — and the buyer who notices the drop first gets the second-chance deal. Facebook gives you no way to watch a listing. Here's how to do it automatically.

The problem: Facebook won't tell you

Marketplace has no price-watch feature. Saving a listing does nothing except bookmark it — if the seller drops the price by $200 tonight, you'll find out whenever you next remember to check, along with everyone else.

Why price drops are a buying signal

A price drop means the seller is getting impatient — the listing didn't move at the old price. That's the moment offers below asking start getting accepted. Sellers who've dropped once often drop again, or take a reasonable cash offer the same day.

Tracking listings with Zoopy's Shortlist

  1. Open any listing Zoopy has caught and tap Shortlist.
  2. Zoopy keeps monitoring it — shortlisted items are re-checked continuously, jumping the queue ahead of ordinary re-checks.
  3. You get a push alert when the price drops, when the listing goes pending, and when it's sold.

The pending/sold signals matter more than they sound: they tell you when to stop waiting, and — across your shortlist — what things actually sell for, which sharpens your sense of a real deal.

Strategy: shortlist anything within 15% of your target price. A drop alert plus a same-day cash offer converts remarkably often — you're negotiating at the exact moment the seller is most flexible.
