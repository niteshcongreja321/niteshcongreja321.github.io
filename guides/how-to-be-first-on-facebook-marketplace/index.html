How to get to new Marketplace listings sooner

Why new Marketplace listings take minutes to appear, and what helps: newest-first searches, tight keywords, alerts and a clear first message. No guarantee.

By the Zoopy team Updated 26 September 2026 7 min read

Quick answer

No tool or trick guarantees you are first on Facebook Marketplace. What helps: sort searches by newest, keep them tight, check at set times or use alerts, and send a specific first message as soon as you see a match. Be ready to pay and collect. Facebook itself keeps new listings out of search for a few minutes after posting.

Can anyone guarantee you are first?

No, and it is worth being clear about why before you spend money or time chasing it.

  • Everyone meets the same delay. A new listing reaches public search for all buyers at about the same time (more on that below).
  • The seller may already have a buyer. A friend, a neighbour or someone from a local group can be lined up before the listing goes up.
  • The seller chooses. Sellers do not have to answer in message order. Some pick whoever sounds most likely to turn up and pay, or whoever can collect soonest.
  • Messages arrive together. When a good listing appears, you may be one of several people writing within the same minute.

So the realistic goal is to get there early and be the easiest buyer to say yes to. The rest of this guide is about both.

Why do new listings take minutes to show in search?

In Zoopy’s measurements around Brisbane in September 2026, Facebook kept a new listing out of public search for about six to seven minutes after the seller posted it. That delay applies to everyone searching: you, other buyers, and every alert tool that reads search results. Facebook does not document it, so treat it as how things worked at the time of writing, not a rule.

What that means for you:

  • Refreshing every few seconds does not get you there sooner. Until the listing is in search, there is nothing to find.
  • An alert that arrives a few minutes after posting is about as early as public search allows. Anything claiming to show you listings seconds after posting is worth questioning.
  • What happens after the listing appears matters more. How quickly you see it, and how good your first message is, are where you can make a difference.

Sort by newest and keep your searches tight

Facebook’s Help Centre says you can sort Marketplace search results by distance or by when the listing was created. To see new listings first:

  1. Search for the item.
  2. On a computer, click Sort by and choose Date listed. In the app, tap Filters and choose the date-listed sort.
  3. Set a price range and a location radius that match what you will really buy and how far you will really drive.

Labels can differ by app version and country.

Then make each search narrow enough that a new result means something:

Instead of Try Why
“drill” “Makita DHP484” and “Makita drill kit” Fewer results, each worth opening
“iPhone” “iPhone 15 Pro 256” with a price range Cuts out cases, screens and parts
“car” in Vehicles Make and model, year range, a maximum price You can act on a match without reading ten others
One wide search Three or four narrow ones Each stays short enough to scan in seconds

Check at set times, or use alerts

You cannot watch Marketplace all day, and you do not need to.

If you check by hand, pick a few times that fit your day and run your saved searches in the same order each time. Nobody publishes reliable data on the best hour to check. Sellers post when they have time, so check when the sellers in your area are likely to be free, such as evenings and weekends.

Facebook’s own notifications. Facebook lets you save a search and switch on notifications for saved searches (the Marketplace notification settings list “saved searches” as a category). They are free and worth turning on. Whether one arrives depends on Facebook and on your phone’s settings; our guide to notifications that are not working covers the settings to check, and the Marketplace alerts guide covers set-up.

Alert apps check your searches on a schedule and push new matches to your phone. If you try one, look at how often it checks, what an alert shows you, and whether its filters read the description or just the title. Our checklist for choosing a Marketplace alert app lists the questions to ask.

Send a first message that gets a reply

Facebook offers a one-tap message that says “Is this available?”, and you can write your own message instead. Sellers get a lot of the one-tap version, and in March 2026 TechCrunch reported that sellers can now let Meta AI draft replies to buyers’ messages automatically. A specific message gives the seller something real to say yes to.

A good first message has three parts: what you want, when you can collect, and how you will pay.

Hi Sam, I’d like the Makita drill kit at $120. I can pick up from Kedron today between 5 and 6 pm and pay by bank transfer once I’ve tested it. Does that work?

Hi, I’m interested in the 2016 Corolla. Is the service book with the car, and is there any finance owing on it? I can inspect tomorrow morning.

Hi, would you take $250 for the bike? I can collect this evening and pay on pickup.

Hi, before I drive over: does the PS5 read discs, and do both controllers work without stick drift? If so, I can come tonight.

What to avoid:

  • “Is this still available?” with nothing else, if you can say more.
  • A lowball offer with no reason and no pickup time.
  • “What’s your lowest price?” before you have seen it.
  • A long story. Keep it to two or three sentences.

If the seller replies, answer quickly and confirm a time. A fast first message is wasted if you then take hours to reply.

Be ready to pay and collect

Getting there early only helps if you can close. Before you message, tick these off:

  • Your top price for this item, written down
  • How you will pay: cash, or a bank transfer you can make on the spot after inspecting
  • Transport that fits it: a boot, roof racks, a trailer, a friend with a ute
  • Time to collect today or tomorrow
  • The checks you will do at pickup, such as testing batteries or checking a phone is not locked to its owner
  • A public, well-lit place to meet if you are not collecting from a home

Two cautions. Do not pay a deposit to “hold” something you have not seen; Scamwatch advises against paying for big-ticket items before inspecting them in person. And if you pay by PayID or bank transfer, check the name matches the seller. Facebook’s own meeting tips also suggest telling someone you trust where you are going and not carrying large amounts of cash.

What does not help, or backfires

  • Refreshing constantly. It does not beat the search delay and it wears you out.
  • Paying a deposit to jump the queue. Scammers ask for exactly this, and you lose the chance to inspect first.
  • Copy-paste lowballs. Sellers tend to ignore them, or remember you.
  • Very broad searches. You spend your head start reading irrelevant results.
  • Waiting to think about it. Decide your price and checks before you message, so you can act when you see a match.

Questions people ask

Is there a way to guarantee being first on Facebook Marketplace?

No. Other buyers can see a listing at the same moment you do, the seller may already have someone lined up, and sellers choose who to deal with. What you control is how soon you look and how easy you are to sell to.

Why don't new listings show up in search straight away?

In Zoopy's measurements, Facebook keeps a new listing out of public search for about six to seven minutes after it is posted, for everyone. Facebook does not document this, and it can change.

How do I sort Facebook Marketplace by newest?

Search for something, then sort by Date listed. On a computer the option is under Sort by; in the app it is under Filters. Labels can differ by app version.

What should my first message say?

Say what you want, when you can collect, and how you will pay, in two or three short sentences. A specific message is easier to say yes to than 'Is this available?'
