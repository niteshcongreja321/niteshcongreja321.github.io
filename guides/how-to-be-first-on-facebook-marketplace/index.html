How to Be First to Every New Facebook Marketplace Listing

Every experienced Marketplace buyer knows the rule: the deal goes to whoever messages first with a credible offer. Underpriced items get 20+ messages in the first hour — sellers usually just take the first sensible one. Being first isn't luck; it's a system.

Why the first message wins

Sellers list, watch the messages roll in, and stop responding after the first few. On genuinely underpriced items the window is minutes, not hours. If you learn about listings from Facebook's own notifications (hours late) or by scrolling (whenever you happen to look), you're structurally last.

The fast buyer's playbook

  1. Automate discovery. Run a monitoring app checking your searches every 15–30 seconds. This is the whole game — everything else is marginal.
  2. Pre-write your opener. "Hi! Is this still available? I can pick up today at [suburb] and pay cash — can do [time]." Concrete beats clever.
  3. Decide your max price in advance. Zoopy shows a valuation against comparable listings on every alert, so you know instantly if the price has margin — no frantic googling while someone else messages first.
  4. Confirm fast, ask later. Lock in the pickup, then ask secondary questions. The buyer who says "yes, today, cash" beats the one asking five questions.

Where Zoopy fits

Zoopy compresses step 1 to seconds: hunts check as often as every 15 seconds on Turbo, alerts land on your lock screen with photo, price, distance and valuation, and junk (dealers, damaged, write-offs, likely scams) never reaches you. Users report messaging sellers while the listing is under a minute old.

Speed ladder: Relaxed (15 min) for casual watching → Standard (60 s) for most hunts → Turbo (15 s) for iPhones, consoles, tools and underpriced cars — the categories where seconds decide the winner.
